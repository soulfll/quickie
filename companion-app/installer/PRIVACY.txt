Quick-Key Companion App -- Privacy Notice

What this app does:
  - Listens in the background for a small set of dedicated keyboard codes
    (F13 through F20) sent by your Quick-Key device. These codes are not used
    by any normal keyboard shortcut, so this app never sees or reacts to
    your regular typing.
  - When one of those keys is pressed, it runs whatever you assigned to it:
    opening an app already installed on this computer, opening a website in
    your default browser, typing a text snippet you wrote yourself, or
    replaying a set of keystrokes you recorded.
  - The one exception to "never sees your regular typing" is the Record
    Keystrokes feature. Only while you have a recording open -- from
    clicking Start Recording until you click Stop -- the app captures the
    keys you press in any program, so it can replay them later. Those
    recordings are saved only in config.json on this computer. Do not type
    passwords or other secrets while recording.
  - Your key assignments are saved in a local file (config.json) inside the
    app's own folder on this computer.

What this app does NOT do:
  - It does not send anything over the internet. No analytics, no telemetry,
    no remote server of any kind.
  - Outside of an active recording, it does not read, log, or transmit your
    regular keystrokes -- only the dedicated F13-F20 codes from the
    physical Quick-Key device are acted on.
  - It does not access your files, browsing history, or any other
    application's data beyond launching what you explicitly configured it
    to launch.

Why it asks for Accessibility / Input Monitoring permission (macOS) or
similar background-input permission (Windows):
  This is required by the operating system for any app that needs to react
  to a key press while another app is focused -- the same category of
  permission used by tools like window-snapping utilities or hardware macro
  pads. It is not a special or unusual request for what this app does.
